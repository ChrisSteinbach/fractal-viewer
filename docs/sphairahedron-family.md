# Sphairahedral limit sets (pre-gate study)

The record behind the proposed Surface family built from inversion in
INTERSECTING spheres — the Ahara–Nakamura sphairahedra
(sphairahedron.net): connected quasi-spheres with hexagram arms and crater
fields, and infinite terrains, rendered as the LIMIT SET of the group
generated by the face involutions. Nothing here is production code. The
prototype math lives in `scripts/sphairahedron-orbit.ts` and the executable
record in `scripts/sphairahedron.harness.ts` (run through
`scripts/sphairahedron-factory.ts`, the `de-preview-parallel` scene factory);
both wait on the owner's visual gate — the epic's promotion gate — before any
`src/` module, shader, persistence or UI work begins.

Run:

```bash
npx vitest run --config scripts/vitest.harness.config.ts scripts/sphairahedron.harness.ts
```

It writes `scripts/out/sphairahedron-finite.png`, `-terrain.png`,
`-anchor.png`, `-orbit.png`, `-points.png` and `-4d.png` in about 40 s across
16 workers at 288 px (`SPHAIRA_SIZE=N` sets the panel size,
`SPHAIRA_SERIAL=1` renders in-thread, `SPHAIRA_DEBUG=1` prints the framing
measurements).

## Sources

All formulas trace to three published sources, read as PDFs converted to
text; the reference implementation (soma-arc/SphairahedronExperiment, GPL-3)
was read for STRUCTURE only — the parameter regions and every construction
number here are re-derived, and the harness re-checks each construction
numerically against the lemmas instead of trusting either source.

- Nakamura & Ahara, Bridges 2018, pp. 171–178 — the families and the first
  IIS-rendered images.
- Nakamura, Araki & Ahara, "Polyhedra with Spherical Faces and
  Four-Dimensional Kleinian Groups", preprint 2020 — the mathematical core:
  Lemmas 3.1/3.3/3.5/3.6, Lemma 3.15, Prop 3.8, Thm 4.3, Def 4.1, the cube
  classification (types 1–11, 6/7/10/11 empty), and the appendix's valid
  regions.
- Nakamura, J. Math. Arts (2021) — the IIS algorithm and the
  Jacobian-division distance estimate.

## The construction

- **Wall system.** Three planes through the height axis whose cross-section
  bounds a triangle; the corner angles are the wall-wall dihedral angles.
  Three systems ship: `333` (equilateral, inradius `h`), `236` and `244`
  (the paper's crystallographic corner sets π/2·π/3·π/6 and π/2·π/4·π/4).
  A wall hyperplane in `n` dims lifts the 2D cross-section normal and leaves
  the height axis (index 1) and the fourth axis (index 3) untouched.
- **Excavation balls.** Ball `b` sits at a wall corner: its ideal vertex is
  the corner point at the ball's own height (Lemma 3.3(1); the ball is
  TANGENT to the vertical corner line there — Lemma 3.3(2) makes it a double
  root), and its center sits at distance `r_b` along the direction making
  angle `θ_{b,wall}` with that wall's SOLID-side normal (Lemma 3.3(3)); the
  two directions close the vertex because `θ_a + θ_b + θ_{ab} = π` (Lemma
  3.1). The center's height is the ball's parameter.
- **Radii.** Lemma 3.5's tangency equation per ball pair sharing a wall:
  `r1·sin θ1w + r2·sin θ2w = (w² + Δh²)/(2w)` with `w` the wall's face
  width. The system is LINEAR in the radii — solved by exact elimination,
  no iteration. Pinned radii (the tetra's ball, the prism's second ball)
  substitute.
- **Verification (the gate every construction passes).** The harness
  re-checks, per construction: Lemma 3.3(2) (center–corner distance =
  radius, measured error 4.4e-16 at the shipped parameters), the
  plane/sphere dihedral via `cos θ = δ/r` (same accuracy), Lemma 3.5 (the
  wall circles tangent at the feet of the ball centers), and the existence
  of the ideal vertices: the mixed ball-ball-wall vertices (the wall-circle
  tangency points) and the three-ball vertex P246 (the three spheres' common
  point where their pairwise circles are mutually tangent — which can
  degenerate to a single tangency point; the check clamps that case). The
  measured angle/tangency errors at every shipped parameter are ≤ 4.5e-16.
- **The tile.** The prism ∧ the divide plane (through the corner vertices)
  minus the balls; the divide's solid side is fixed by mapping a probe
  point known to sit deep in the tile. The tile SDF is the max-form
  intersection (walls `a·p − h`, removed balls `r − |p−c|`, divide by the
  probe side) — the reference implementation's exact convention
  (`DistInfSpheirahedra`), sign-negative inside.
- **Valid regions** (the preprint's appendix; the two reference-code
  discrepancies the epic names — CubeE's region and CubeH's third
  inequality — are NOT used): type 1: `zA·zB < 3/4 ∧ zA² − zA·zB < 3/4 ∧
zB² − zA·zB < 3/4` with the symmetry reduction `zB ≥ 2zA` and the cusp
  `(√6/4, √6/2)`; types 4 and 9: the appendix's three hyperbola/line
  inequalities each. Shipped parameters were checked inside; the past-cusp
  panel deliberately renders OUTSIDE the region (the paper's Fig 24: arms
  overlap, meaningful shapes persist).
- **Finite type.** The infinite scene inverted in one more sphere `J` (the
  free parameter): every face maps to a sphere (a face whose surface passes
  through `J`'s center maps to a plane — handled, not refused), and each
  image's solid side is fixed by mapping the probe. The finite faces carry
  a PER-FACE sense: a face whose solid side is the sphere's interior folds
  points that are OUTSIDE it. The framing ball = the divide plane's image
  (the quasi-sphere's smooth carrier) grown to hold the dent spheres.
- **Seeds (the orbit-of-balls view).** The maximum vertex balls of Def 4.1:
  per vertex, the ball through it orthogonal to every edge there (its
  center on the common edge-tangent line — all three edge tangents are
  verified parallel, the ideality check), maximal (touching the nearest
  other vertex along its center line), on the side that pokes into the
  tile. The tetra's vertex balls degenerate to half-spaces (the other
  vertices all lie on the orthogonal sphere's exterior for every center
  position), so the tetra renders through the tile DE only; the cube yields
  6 seeds, the prism 2–3. Thm 4.3's covering hypothesis is exercised by the
  orbit cross-check below.

## The estimator (the reference's, re-measured here)

The IIS fold: per pass, scan the faces in order (balls then walls for the
infinite types; the reference's interleaved order for finite ones); a face
sphere holding the point on its REMOVED side inverts it (`λ ×= R²/|p−c|²`),
a violated wall reflects it (`λ` unchanged); the scan continues within the
pass and passes repeat until a clean one or the cap (50, the reference's
default — a numerical guard, not an object parameter). Estimate =
`tileSDF(folded)/|λ|·fudge` — positive outside the union of tiles, negative
inside, zero set = the fold faces plus their group images.

THIS IS THE KIFS HEURISTIC SHAPE, not a certified bound: the disjoint
family's transported-covering argument leans on disjointness and does not
carry, and the disclosure discipline is the escape-time family's (a
measured step scale, an overshoot instrument, "a distance threshold is not
a membership oracle"), not the disjoint family's certification discipline.
Scan order is PART of the algorithm once faces intersect (the geometry no
longer decides which face folds first).

## Measurements (all from the harness's printed tables)

- **Construction verification:** tangent/angle errors ≤ 4.5e-16 at every
  shipped parameter of types 1, 4, 9, the tetra, and the prism (both
  chiralities and z2 = ±1, ±2).
- **Anchor exactness (the zero set, marching bias refined away by
  bisection to ±1e-13 in ray parameter):** the infinite tetra's limit set
  is the plane y = 0 — the DE's zero crossings measure rms 7.7e-4, max
  2.3e-2 (140 px); the finite tetra's limit set is the image of that plane
  under `J` — an analytic sphere through `J`'s center — rms 5.3e-5, max
  1.3e-3. The 4D tetra lift (the same loop one dimension up, sliced at
  `w = 0` and `w = 0.15`): rms 4.3e-5 and 6.3e-16 respectively — the
  off-slice case is EXACT, the slice of the analytic 3-sphere recovered to
  machine precision. The estimator's zero set IS the analytic limit set.
- **Fold depth at the surface** (12,270 hit points of the canonical
  finite cube at 160 px): concentrated at 5–9 moves (peak 7 at 31.8%),
  tail to 17, cap never reached; ball-sampled queries (mostly outside the
  union) fold in ≤ 1 move, cap 0.00%.
- **Cost:** a surface DE eval (fold + tile SDF) is ~0.5 µs; a 256 px frame
  at 200 steps/ray marches in ~7 s single-thread, ~1 s across the worker
  pool. Surface marching costs 12–29 DE evals/ray across the beauty panels
  (288 px) — the fold is CHEAP.
- **Fudge sweep** (the tetra anchor, 140 px): steps/ray 40 → 26 → 16 → 9 →
  4.4 for fudge 0.05/0.1/0.2/0.4/0.8 at hit rates 80/77/75/74/73%. The
  reference's fudge 0.2 sits at the knee; the study ships 0.2 with march
  step scale 1 (the shipped marcher's damping is the fudge, exactly the
  reference's marching arithmetic).
- **Tile cross-check (the orbit instrument):** the fold DE's hit points
  against the seed-ball orbit (BFS over the Cayley graph, reduced words,
  image spheres): mean distance to the nearest depth-D image sphere DROPS
  9.7e-2 → 5.7e-2 → 3.4e-2 as D goes 2 → 3 → 4 (5622 spheres) — the fold's
  limit set sits on the orbit's accumulation boundary, converging as Thm
  4.3's enumeration predicts. The residual is the depth truncation (the
  deep cusps' gaps), not a disagreement: at D = 4 the orbit sheet and the
  fold DE share the same silhouette.
- **Points stance:** the inverse-iteration random walk (uniform face choice
  per step, keeping points whose fold from there is ≥ 24 moves deep)
  scatters 1.2e5 points onto the limit set; sampled cloud points read
  mean |de| 1.1e-9 (max 1.2e-8) — the cloud IS on the DE's zero set. The
  per-point cost is one inversion + one fold probe; a depth-pruned DFS
  sampler was judged unnecessary for the stance decision (the random walk
  already covers the set's silhouette; revisit at the Points-consumer
  child if coverage proves uneven).
- **Termination outside the region:** the past-cusp parameters (0.65, 1.3)
  render with the same fold statistics as interior ones — the parabolic
  degeneracy the preprint predicts ON the region boundary did not produce
  cap-runaway at the shipped samples (the arms overlap visually instead,
  matching the paper's Fig 24).

## Framing and the inversion sphere (the study's own decisions, disclosed)

The inversion sphere is a FREE parameter; the reference constrains its
default to the mirror of the last ball. That default's center sits on the
TILED side of the divide plane, where the compact side of the limit set is
a complement pocket far too small to put a camera in (measured: the pocket
is < 0.1% of the framing ball's volume). The study reflects the center
through the divide plane (radius kept) so the compact union faces the
camera — the site's gallery presentation. A deterministic picker then
validates the J: it samples the DE sign at the inverted construction's own
scale and accepts the first candidate whose union is a bounded blob
(minority side, ≥ 12 samples), trying the reflected reference, radius
variants, a ladder stepped along the divide's complement normal, and a
ladder over the divide centroid at the wall triangle's scale. Framing is
measured, not derived: the compact side's 92nd-percentile radius × 1.18,
camera on the other side, with a dense re-sample pass when the compact
side registers fewer than 300 samples. Capped folds are excluded from
every sign reading (a capped fold's tile SDF is not a membership reading).
Type 9's reflected J collapses the image construction for every parameter
tried (union ≤ 1 sample); (0.3, 0.2) works through the picker's radius
variants. All of this is rendering stance, not mathematics — the
quasi-sphere deforms continuously with J, and the panels disclose which J
rule each used.

## 4D stance

The fold, the inversion algebra and the estimators are dimension-free and
run at n = 4 unchanged. The study's 4D cell is the tetra lift: the 333
wall system's hyperplanes (the A2 cross-section extended flat along the
extra axis), the unit 3-ball at the origin, the tile cut by the invariant
hyperplane y = 0. Its limit set is the image of that hyperplane — an
analytic 3-sphere once finite — and the anchor above measures the slice
DE's zero set against it to 4.3e-5 (in-slice) and 6.3e-16 (off-slice).
The tiling's discreteness for the 4D cell is NOT claimed: the preprint's
own 4D classification is open (it builds S³ = ∂H⁴ group theory and
classifies nothing in dimension 4), and the regular ideal 4-simplex trap
(dihedral angle arccos(1/3), not a submultiple of π) is avoided only
because the tetra lift's ridges keep the 3D cross-section's π/3 angles.
The panel is exploration evidence that the loop lifts, exactly the bead's
option (a) scoped as exploration, not classification. The slice-then-invert
idioms of the disjoint family do NOT transfer here — there is no echo to
project; a 4D sphairahedron is its own construction.
